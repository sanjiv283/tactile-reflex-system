# Force calibration guide

**Status: procedure and tooling only. No calibration measurement exists for this project.** Every number in the RTL (`FORCE_MAX` = 2560, `FORCE_REL` = 2304 ADC counts) is
an untuned default, not a physical limit. The RTL has no conversion to newtons. Until this guide has been carried out with the real sensor and mechanism, the force limit has
no physical meaning.

Tooling: `tools/calibrate.py` (analysis, tested on synthetic data only: `tests/test_calibrate.py`). It never invents data and never edits the RTL.

## 1. What you are calibrating

The raw 12-bit ADC count, read through the real chain (sensor, divider, ADC, SPI, FPGA), against a known force. You want, at the mechanical force you must never exceed
(`limit_N`): the count at which that force occurs, how repeatable it is, how much hysteresis there is between loading and unloading, and whether the chain saturates first.
A force-sensing resistor is not a precision sensor: repeatability, hysteresis, drift and dependence on where and how the load is applied are large and **must be measured, not assumed**.

## 2. Before you start (safety and setup)

* No motor and no driver connected, supply for the driver off. The calibration is of the sensing chain only.
* A rigid fixture that places a **known mass** on the sensor centred and flat, the same way the gripper will load it (same contact surface and pad). Calibrated masses or a
  reference force gauge; record their certificates. A different contact geometry gives a different curve.
* The board running the real RTL and the real ADC wiring. Use the host logger without AI so nothing else interferes:
  `python3 host/ai_host.py --port <port> --no-ai --log run.csv` (the `force` column is the ADC count the FPGA uses). Pins must first be verified (`tools/check_pins.py`).
* Warm up the electronics for the same time you will use in service; note ambient temperature.

## 3. Procedure

1. Choose at least **5 distinct loads** from zero up to **above** `limit_N` (the tool refuses a limit above the largest load: that would be an extrapolation). Space them to put
   several points near the limit.
2. At each load, in the **up** direction (load added), wait for the reading to settle for a fixed time, then record at least **3 independent readings** (re-seat the load
   between readings; do not just take 3 consecutive samples of one placement).
3. Repeat in the **down** direction (load removed in the same steps) so that hysteresis can be estimated. The tool works without it but then assumes zero hysteresis and says so.
4. Enter one row per reading in a CSV with the exact header `trial,direction,mass_g,adc` (`python3 tools/calibrate.py --make-template calib.csv`), `direction` = `up` or `down`,
   `mass_g` the mass resting on the sensor, `adc` the raw count (0 to 4095). Keep the raw logs.
5. Repeat the whole sweep on another day / after reassembly: the second sweep tells you about drift and re-seating, which a single sweep cannot.

## 4. Analysis

```bash
python3 tools/calibrate.py --data calib.csv --limit-n 20 --margin-sigma 3 --out results/calibration_report.json
```
(Replace 20 with your limit in newtons.) The tool checks the data (header, ranges, at least 5 loads and 3 readings per load, monotonic curve beyond noise, limit inside the
measured range) and reports: mean and standard deviation per load, the count at the limit, repeatability as a percent of the range, hysteresis in counts, sensitivity at the limit
in counts per newton, and a recommendation:

```
FORCE_MAX = counts(limit) - margin_sigma * sigma - hysteresis/2 - 1
FORCE_REL = FORCE_MAX - max(hysteresis, 4*sigma, 5% of the used range, 32)
```
so that the trip happens at or before the limit with the stated margin. It warns when the ADC reaches the rail (the RTL treats readings at or above `RAIL_HI` = 0xFF0 as invalid),
when `FORCE_MAX` is within 80 counts of the rail, when sensitivity at the limit is poor, and when no down-data were given.

## 5. Judgement the tool cannot make

* Whether a 3-sigma margin is enough for your hazard. The recommendation covers measurement scatter and hysteresis only. It does **not** include reaction time (the force keeps
  rising for the time between trip and mechanical stop), mechanical compliance, load rate effects, temperature, or sensor drift.
* Whether the limit itself is right. The force at which something is hurt or damaged comes from the application, not from this repository.
* The force-to-command LUT (`rtl/force_lut.mem`, generated by `tools/gen_lut.py`) is a design curve over counts and is **not** calibrated by this procedure.

## 6. Applying the result (manual, deliberate)

1. Edit `FORCE_MAX` / `FORCE_REL` (and, if you change the scale, `PRED_MIN_DELTA` and the predictor window) in `rtl/safety_governor.v`; keep `FORCE_REL < FORCE_MAX`.
2. The testbenches and experiments assume the default thresholds in several places (`tb/`, `experiments/`, `ml/pred_eval.py`, `tools/check_wokwi.py`). Update them to the new values
   and re-run `sh sim/run_all.sh`, `python3 tools/mutation_test.py` and `python3 tools/check_traceability.py`. A threshold change is an RTL change: nothing carries over automatically.
3. Re-verify on the bench (`HARDWARE_VALIDATION_GUIDE.md`, stage 7): at the new thresholds, load to just below and above the limit with the masses and check the trip.
4. Keep the CSV, the JSON report, the mass certificates, the date, the temperature and the RTL commit together.

## 7. Acceptance criteria to write down before measuring

Decide in advance, and record: the maximum acceptable scatter at the limit (counts), the maximum drift between two sweeps, the minimum sensitivity at the limit, and who signs off.
If any criterion fails, the sensor/divider/mechanics must change, not the criteria.

## 8. Not done

Everything in sections 2 to 6: **BLOCKED** (no sensor, fixture or masses in the development environment). The tool itself is tested only on a made-up saturating curve with noise.
